# 04 — Steering

## Architecture

Use conventional kart direct steering:

- Steering wheel and 16–20 mm steel steering shaft supported by two bearings.
- Welded/bolted pitman arm at the lower shaft.
- Two adjustable tie rods with quality spherical joints.
- Kart spindles mounted in double-shear C-brackets.
- Positive mechanical steering stops on both sides.

A complete kart steering kit is often safer and faster than fabricating spindles from scratch. Verify spindle diameter, wheel bearings and tie-rod threads before ordering.

## Initial geometry targets

- Caster: 8–10 degrees positive.
- Camber: approximately 0 degrees static.
- Kingpin inclination: 8–12 degrees, dependent on spindle design.
- Total static toe: 0 to 2 mm toe-out.
- Maximum inner-wheel steering angle: approximately 28–32 degrees.
- Steering-wheel rotation: approximately 90–120 degrees each side of centre.

These are starting values, not certified geometry. Final settings depend on tyre diameter, spindle offset, wheelbase and track.

## Ackermann layout

In plan view, lines extended through the steering arms should approximately meet near the centre of the rear axle. For a wheelbase `L`, front track `T` and inner-wheel angle `delta_i`, the ideal outer-wheel angle is:

```text
tan(delta_o) = L / (L / tan(delta_i) + T)
```

For L = 1,100 mm, T = 820 mm and inner angle 30 degrees, the ideal outer angle is about 21.7 degrees.

## Mock-up procedure before welding brackets

1. Place the actual front wheels at intended track and ground clearance.
2. Clamp temporary kingpin brackets; do not weld.
3. Set caster/camber with digital angle gauge.
4. Point steering arms toward rear-axle centre.
5. Connect tie rods and cycle full travel with the seat occupied.
6. Confirm tyres, tie rods and steering arms do not touch frame or pedals.
7. Add mechanical stops before final welding.
8. Perform bump/side-load inspection; no rod end should bind at full lock.

## Fastener retention

Use prevailing-torque locknuts or castellated nuts with cotter pins on steering joints. Paint-mark every critical nut after final torque. Do not rely on thread-locker alone for a joint whose separation causes loss of steering.
