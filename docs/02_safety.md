# 02 — Safety concept and hazard analysis

## Mandatory operating rules

1. Closed private property only.
2. One responsible adult controls the key and emergency stop.
3. Properly fitted full-face helmet, closed shoes, gloves and long clothing.
4. No passenger, towing, jumps, wet operation or driving near traffic, water, walls, stairs or parked vehicles.
5. No child drives until an adult has completed every commissioning test.
6. Charge the battery outside the kart or with the traction system isolated; never charge unattended.

## Major hazards and controls

| Hazard | Consequence | Required controls |
|---|---|---|
| Stuck throttle | Collision | Dual return springs, idle switch/plausibility logic, brake-throttle override, parent E-stop, mechanical brake |
| Controller short or software fault | Uncommanded torque | Main contactor, current limit, parent key, guarded test stand, conservative modes |
| Battery short circuit | Fire, burns | DC fuse near battery, insulated enclosure, strain relief, covered terminals, correct cable size |
| Chain/sprocket contact | Entanglement, laceration | Full fixed guard requiring tools to remove |
| Brake failure | Collision | Hydraulic mechanical brake sized for full vehicle; regen supplemental only; pre-run inspection |
| Steering lock or separation | Loss of control | Locknuts/castle nuts and cotter pins, mechanical stops, double-shear mounts, witness marks |
| Rollover | Head/neck injury | Low centre of gravity, 25 km/h cap, no abrupt steering tests, wide track, no seat belt without roll structure |
| Hot motor/controller | Burn or fire | Temperature monitoring, derating, guarded components, post-run inspection |
| Child changes mode | Excess speed | Parent-only key switch; key removed when parked |
| Radio remote loss | No remote stop | Fail-safe receiver that inhibits torque on signal loss; local E-stop remains primary |

## Emergency-stop philosophy

The brake pedal first commands zero torque and optional mild regenerative braking. The large red emergency stop opens the main contactor as a last-resort traction isolation. Because opening the contactor also removes electrical braking, it must never be treated as the normal brake.

## Seat belt decision

Do not fit a belt to an open kart without a roll cage. A belt can hold the driver in the crush zone during a rollover. If a future roll structure is engineered, the belt, seat, mounting points, head clearance and rollover geometry must be designed together.

## Inspection intervals

- Before every session: steering fasteners, brake fluid/leaks, pedal return, chain tension, guards, tyre condition, battery retention and E-stop.
- After the first 10, 30 and 60 minutes: re-torque all critical fasteners and inspect welds.
- Every 5 operating hours: remove guards, inspect sprockets, chain elongation, axle keys, brake pads and cable abrasion.
